\section{Discussion}

The experiments support a hierarchy of aggregate supervision.
The clearest gains occur when the observed aggregate is not well represented by only an expectation or a class proportion.
Scalar expected-value matching is weak in low-data digit-sum settings, whereas exact aggregate likelihood can recover accurate instance predictors from the same scalar labels.
With more training bags and small bags, expected-value baselines can become competitive, so the supported claim is data efficiency and likelihood fidelity rather than universal dominance.
The SVHN-sum and UltraMNIST sum benchmarks strengthen the more basic atomic-support claim: the same aggregator remains useful when the support is fixed at $S=10$ but the image domain and backbone change.
Together with binary and signed benchmarks, these results support the view that the method is a generic finite-support sum-label layer rather than a collection of task-specific losses.
Histogram labels are substantially richer, and standard LLP/PVC-style baselines can already be strong.
On MNIST histogram supervision, however, exact one-vs-rest count likelihood improves over CE/KL proportion matching, especially for larger bags, which suggests that likelihood fidelity still matters even when the aggregate label is rich.
This distinction is important for positioning: our goal is not to replace every LLP method, but to provide a unified likelihood framework for finite-support aggregate labels beyond ordinary binary counts and class proportions.
The same formulation is also modular with respect to the instance model.
Backbone choice determines the quality of the atomic PMFs, while the aggregate likelihood is determined by the weak label: binary counts, signed counts, ordinal sums, and one-vs-rest histograms all use the same convolutional bag-prediction interface.
This modularity is useful computationally as well as conceptually, because the exact aggregate PMF can be evaluated by CPU DP, direct convolution, grouped convolution, or GPU FFT-tree multiplication without changing the statistical objective.

Several limitations remain.
Large bags can expose calibration issues even when instance ranking is accurate.
Cancellation-heavy signed aggregates are genuinely ambiguous and require more data.
CIFAR-scale experiments require matching the backbone initialization and training protocol used by recent LLP work.
Pretrained ResNet-18 PVC is effective for $n=16$ and $n=64$, but the fixed-instance-budget comparison shows that the apparent large-bag gain disappears when total sampled image exposure is matched.
These results therefore support a cautious claim about effective use of aggregate-labeled image volume under a strong representation, not a stronger claim about intrinsic large-bag superiority or superiority to fully supervised learning at matched annotation budget.
