\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments support \method{} as a common likelihood layer for finite-support aggregate supervision.
Its strongest gains appear when the aggregate label is poorly summarized by a mean or proportion, as in low-data digit sums and signed counts with cancellation.
The Bernoulli and classwise count experiments primarily establish continuity with prior count-likelihood work: they show that the same interface includes prior count likelihoods and one-vs-rest count pseudo-likelihoods, not that ordinary count loss or full joint histogram LLP is new.

The main limitations are ambiguity and representation dependence.
Large scalar-sum bags can admit many latent assignments with the same aggregate value, and signed aggregates make this non-identifiability explicit through cancellation.
CIFAR-scale results also depend on pretrained features and sampled image exposure; the fixed-instance-budget comparison shows that larger bags are not intrinsically better.

Future work will use this finite-support signed-count view in the mathematical setting that motivated part of the project.
Appendix~\ref{app:qtlr-origin} sketches how signed aggregate labels arise from conjectural factorizations of $(q,t)$-Littlewood--Richardson coefficients.
Developing that application requires problem-specific architectures and diagnostics beyond the scope of this paper, but it is a natural next test of whether exact aggregate likelihoods can recover latent combinatorial structure.
