\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The experiments support a hierarchy of aggregate supervision.
The largest gains occur when the observed aggregate is not well represented by only an expectation or a class proportion.
Scalar expected-value matching is weak in low-data digit-sum settings, whereas exact aggregate likelihood can recover accurate instance predictors from the same scalar labels.
With more training bags and small bags, expected-value baselines can become competitive, so the observed advantage is data efficiency and likelihood fidelity rather than universal dominance.
The SVHN and UltraMNIST results strengthen the digit-sum evidence: the same integer-valued sum likelihood remains useful when the support is fixed at $S=10$ but the image domain and backbone change.
The signed FashionMNIST and CIFAR-10 probes make the same point for cancellation aggregates: the method is not tied to MNIST digits or to nonnegative counts.
Together with binary, ordinal, signed, and histogram benchmarks, these results support the view that \method{} is a finite-support aggregate-likelihood layer rather than a collection of task-specific losses.

Histogram labels are substantially richer, and standard LLP objectives can already be strong.
We include the histogram setting mainly to connect the finite-support view to LLP-PVC-style one-vs-rest count likelihoods.
Our main comparison shows that exact classwise count likelihood improves over MSE expected-count matching, while we do not claim a new full joint histogram likelihood or a reproduction of full LLP-PVC.

The same formulation is modular with respect to the instance model.
Backbone choice determines the quality of the atomic PMFs, while the aggregate likelihood is determined by the weak label: binary counts, signed counts, integer-valued sums, and one-vs-rest histograms all use the same convolutional bag-prediction interface.
This modularity is useful computationally as well as conceptually, because the exact aggregate PMF can be evaluated by CPU DP, direct convolution, grouped convolution, or GPU FFT-tree multiplication without changing the statistical objective.

Several limitations remain.
Large bags can expose calibration issues even when instance ranking is accurate.
Signed aggregates can be ambiguous because different latent assignments may yield the same observed sum.
CIFAR-scale experiments require matching the backbone initialization and training protocol used by recent LLP work.
Pretrained ResNet-18 one-vs-rest count likelihood is effective for $n=16$ and $n=64$, but the fixed-instance-budget comparison shows that the large-bag gain disappears when total sampled image exposure is matched.
The CIFAR signed-count result is currently a single-seed probe, so it supports feasibility on natural images but should not be read as a final benchmark claim.
These results show effective use of aggregate-labeled image volume under a strong representation, but they do not indicate intrinsic large-bag superiority or superiority to fully supervised learning at matched annotation budget.
