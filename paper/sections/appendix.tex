\section{Implementation Details}
\label{app:implementation-details}

All weak-supervision experiments sample bags on the fly rather than pregenerating fixed bag files.
Instance labels are used only for evaluation metrics such as hidden-instance AUC or hidden digit accuracy.
MNIST-family experiments use LeNet-style instance classifiers, Adam with learning rate $5\times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, and 50 epochs.
The main MNIST-family configurations use seeds $0,1,2$, test on 1000 sampled bags, and evaluate mean bag sizes 10 and 50 with standard deviations 2 and 10.

\paragraph{Backend implementation.}
Sequential finite-support convolution accumulates shifted copies of the current aggregate PMF and is used for the moderate-size binary, signed, and integer-valued sum experiments.
Dynamic programming is used as a Shukla-style Bernoulli count baseline and computes the same binary count coefficients as the convolutional formulation.
For grouped signed binary aggregates, each active group starts as a point mass at zero on support $[-K_{\max},K_{\max}]$.
For one signed instance, the grouped GPU backend applies a length-three channelwise kernel and padding one.
Because PyTorch's \texttt{conv1d} operator is cross-correlation rather than mathematical convolution, the implementation stores the kernel in the order
\begin{equation}
  [p_i\mathbf{1}\{s_i=+1\},\;1-p_i,\;p_i\mathbf{1}\{s_i=-1\}],
\end{equation}
so the left kernel entry shifts probability mass toward larger aggregate values and the right entry shifts mass toward smaller values.
This is only an implementation orientation; in mathematical support order $[-1,0,+1]$ the signed kernels are the atoms described in Section~\ref{sec:convolutional-bag-prediction}.

For digit-sum, the atom width is the number of finite digit values, for example ten for MNIST digits.
For histogram experiments, we avoid a full $C$-dimensional joint histogram over classes and instead compute $C$ one-vs-rest one-dimensional count likelihoods in parallel.
This keeps the aggregate support one-dimensional for each class and makes large bags practical on GPU.
The FFT-tree backend is useful for this setting because the same pairwise polynomial products are batched over classes and bags.
Padding or masked positions are represented by deterministic zero-contribution atoms, such as $[1,0]$ for Bernoulli counts, $[0,1,0]$ for signed support $[-1,0,+1]$, and $[1,0,\ldots,0]$ for integer-valued sums.

\paragraph{Histogram and CIFAR settings.}
Histogram count-likelihood experiments treat each class count as a one-vs-rest Bernoulli count likelihood.
The MNIST histogram experiments use batch size 192 for large-bag configurations.
CIFAR-10 experiments use ResNet-18 backbones.
The pretrained one-vs-rest count settings use ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 with random crop and horizontal flip augmentation, SGD with momentum 0.9, weight decay $10^{-4}$, learning rate $5\times 10^{-4}$, and a cosine learning-rate schedule for 500 epochs.
The pretrained CIFAR-10 analysis is limited to the evaluated $n=16$ and $n=64$ one-vs-rest count settings.

\section{Mathematical origin: signed counting in \texorpdfstring{$(q,t)$}{(q,t)}-Littlewood--Richardson coefficients}
\label{app:qtlr-origin}

The signed-count instance of our framework was originally motivated by a problem in algebraic combinatorics: a conjectural factorization of the $(q,t)$-Littlewood--Richardson coefficients arising in the theory of Macdonald polynomials~\cite{Macdonald1995}.
This object is central across several areas of mathematics---symmetric function theory, representation theory, Schubert calculus on Grassmannians, and partition combinatorics---and it also surfaces in mathematical physics through connections to the Calogero--Sutherland model and related integrable systems~\cite{LittlewoodRichardson1934,KnutsonTao1999,Vakil2006}.
We sketch how this problem reduces to signed counting in our framework; complete mathematical exposition and the corresponding empirical study fall outside the scope of this paper.

\paragraph{Setup.}
A partition $\lambda=(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_k)$ is identified with its Young diagram, a finite collection of unit cells.
For a cell $u$ in $\lambda$, the \emph{arm length} $a_\lambda(u)$ is the number of cells strictly to the right of $u$ in the same row, and the \emph{leg length} $\ell_\lambda(u)$ is the number of cells strictly below $u$ in the same column.
Figure~\ref{fig:qtlr-partition} illustrates these quantities on the partition $(4,3,1)$.
The atomic building block of the theory is the rational function
\begin{equation}
b(a,\ell) \;:=\; \frac{1-q^{a}\,t^{\ell+1}}{1-q^{a+1}\,t^{\ell}},
\qquad
b_\lambda(u;q,t) \;:=\; b\bigl(a_\lambda(u),\,\ell_\lambda(u)\bigr).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The conjectural factorization.}
The Littlewood--Richardson coefficient $c^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$~\cite{LittlewoodRichardson1934} admits a $(q,t)$-refinement $f^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)$, a rational function in $q,t$ with $f^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}(0,0)=c^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}$.
A long-standing conjecture of Stanley~\cite{Stanley1989} predicts that whenever $c^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}=1$, there exist subsets
$X^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\subseteq\lambda$, $Y^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\subseteq\mu$, and $Z^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\subseteq\nu$ such that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stanley-factorization}
f^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)
\;=\;
\frac{\prod_{u\in Z^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}} b_\nu(u;q,t)}
     {\prod_{u\in X^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}} b_\lambda(u;q,t)\,\cdot\,\prod_{u\in Y^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}} b_\mu(u;q,t)}.
\end{equation}
The conjecture remains open in general; the case where $\mu$ is a single-row partition is known explicitly as the \emph{Pieri rule}~\cite[p.~340]{Macdonald1995}.

\paragraph{Reduction to signed finite-support aggregation.}
Two observations connect Eq.~\eqref{eq:stanley-factorization} to signed bag-level counting.
First, the atomic factor $b_\lambda(u;q,t)$ depends on the cell $u$ only through its arm--leg pair $(a_\lambda(u),\ell_\lambda(u))$, so cells from different partitions or different positions but sharing the same value $(a,\ell)$ all contribute the same factor $b(a,\ell)$ to $f^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)$, and may therefore be grouped together.
Second, the conjectural subsets enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:stanley-factorization} with opposite multiplicative roles: cells in $Z^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\subseteq\nu$ appear in the numerator, while cells in $X^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\subseteq\lambda$ and $Y^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\subseteq\mu$ appear in the denominator.
Letting $+1$ (resp.\ $-1$) signify a numerator (resp.\ denominator) contribution and grouping cells by arm--leg type, the multiplicities collapse into a single signed integer exponent per type, yielding
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lr-as-signed-count}
f^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)
\;=\;
\prod_{(a,\ell)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{2}} b(a,\ell)^{\beta_{\lambda,\mu,\nu,a,\ell}},
\qquad
\beta_{\lambda,\mu,\nu,a,\ell}
\;=\;
\sum_{u\in\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu,a,\ell}} s_u\,z_u.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{B}_{\lambda,\mu,\nu,a,\ell}=\{u\in\lambda\sqcup\mu\sqcup\nu : (a_u,\ell_u)=(a,\ell)\}$ is the arm--leg bag, the sign is $s_u=+1$ for $u\in\nu$ and $s_u=-1$ for $u\in\lambda\sqcup\mu$, and $z_u\in\{0,1\}$ indicates whether the cell $u$ belongs to the conjectural selected subset $X^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\cup Y^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}\cup Z^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}$.
The exponents $\beta_{\lambda,\mu,\nu,a,\ell}$ are read off from the rational expression of $f^\nu_{\lambda,\mu}(q,t)$, while the underlying selections $\{z_u\}$ are unobserved and need not be unique: distinct latent selections can produce the same vector of observed exponents, exactly the cancellation phenomenon that distinguishes signed counting from plain Bernoulli counting.

Identifying instances with cells, bags with arm--leg equivalence classes, the aggregate label with the observed exponent $\beta_{\lambda,\mu,\nu,a,\ell}$, and the latent selection with membership in the conjectural subset, Eq.~\eqref{eq:lr-as-signed-count} is exactly the signed finite-support aggregate-likelihood setting of Section~\ref{sec:problem-setup}.
Each triple $(\lambda,\mu,\nu)$ produces a family of such bags indexed by $(a,\ell)$, each with a signed integer label, and the convolutional bag-prediction layer of the main paper applies without modification.

\paragraph{Empirical note.}
In preliminary experiments using the framework of this paper, we observed over $98\%$ bag-level exponent accuracy on out-of-distribution Pieri- and Stanley-type triples, indicating that exact signed-count likelihood can recover latent cell selections consistent with the conjectural factorization.
The detailed mathematical formulation, problem-specific architectural choices, and combinatorial diagnostics are outside the scope of the present paper and are left to a separate report.
